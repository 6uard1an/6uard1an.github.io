Amudi Privacy Policy

Effective and last updated: November 23, 2026

This policy applies to the Amudi Android app and Amudi web reader. MEIR DAVID EDEN BAR SELA, trading as Amudi, is the developer and data controller for the account and sync features described here. Questions may be sent to guardian.thisdevice@gmail.com.

What Amudi stores

  • On your device: downloaded books, bookmarks, notes, reading progress, reader settings, source sheets, learning-plan choices, reminder settings, and local diagnostic preferences.
  • Offline book access: updated app versions keep a local list of recently server-verified book product IDs and any verified full-library tester grant, separated by Amudi account ID. This lets the same account read books it already downloaded without a connection for up to seven days after verification. Reconnect to renew that check; a refund or other revocation takes effect when the app reconnects, or when the offline check expires. Signing into another account does not transfer access. Deleting the account or local personal data removes this device copy.
  • In your private cloud account when you sign in: your Firebase user ID, email address, optional Google display name and profile photo, plus a synchronized snapshot of bookmarks, notes, progress, settings, source sheets, verified book-ownership entitlements, and related update times.
  • For book purchases: Google Play processes the payment. Amudi receives a product identifier, purchase token, purchase state, and an account-binding value so its secure verifier can confirm the purchase and save ownership to the correct Amudi account. Amudi does not receive your card or bank-account details. If Google Play reports a purchase as refunded, cancelled, or charged back, Amudi’s verifier removes that book’s ownership from the linked account and keeps a record of the voided receipt (a purchase-token hash, the Google Play order ID, and the time and reason Google reports) so the same receipt cannot unlock the book again.
  • For tester access: the group owner verifies approved Amudi Alpha Testers membership. Amudi stores SHA-256 hashes of normalized Google email addresses, permanent access grants, and signed account claims. At Google sign-in, Amudi’s authenticated server may ask the owner-authorized Google Apps Script to check that account’s current membership. The script returns a membership result and email hash; no scheduled polling runs. Accounts with a permanent grant are not checked against group membership again. Pending and banned memberships do not receive new grants. Previously granted access is permanent.
  • For account security and delivery: Firebase and Google services may process authentication events, IP address, device/app metadata, integrity or App Check tokens, and service logs. GitHub receives ordinary request data when the app downloads library packages. If the primary library catalog request fails, Amudi may request the catalog through jsDelivr, which also receives ordinary request data such as the IP address. Some installed versions automatically contact Time.Now when online, at most once per day, to check the device clock and infer a time zone from the request IP address. This request occurs before any prompt about whether to use the suggested time or zone. A prepared app update removes that automatic request; installed versions may retain it until updated.
  • For Daf Study: when you open a supported daf with Daf Study enabled, the reader may request that daf's study information from talmud.dev. The Android app contacts talmud.dev directly. The web reader first tries a direct request; if browser cross-origin rules block it, the web reader sends the daf reference through an Amudi-operated Cloudflare Worker, which retrieves the response from talmud.dev. The request does not include your Amudi account token, notes, or bookmarks. The service receiving a request can process ordinary network data such as IP address and browser or app metadata. Older web-reader copies may still use the public proxies cors.tools, proxy.cors.dev, or api.allorigins.win until updated. Those services have their own data practices.
  • Reminders: Android reminders are scheduled locally. Web push is disabled unless configured and enabled by the user; if enabled, the push endpoint, chosen time, time zone, language, and study-program identifier are sent to the configured reminder service.

Why the data is used

Amudi uses this information to authenticate users, synchronize their reading data across devices, verify purchases, save and restore book ownership, deliver books, restore settings, schedule requested reminders, protect the library service, diagnose failures, and respond to support or deletion requests. Amudi does not sell personal data and does not use it for targeted advertising.

Services that receive data

Amudi uses Google Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, App Check/Play Integrity, and Google sign-in; Google Play Billing and the Google Play Developer API for purchases; Amudi-operated Cloudflare Workers for purchase verification and browser Daf Study delivery; GitHub Releases and raw GitHub content for library delivery, with jsDelivr as a catalog fallback; talmud.dev for Daf Study; Hebcal for calendar-based study information; and, in versions with the clock check, Time.Now. Versions with bundled calendar schedules do not contact Hebcal to show those schedules; earlier installed versions may request schedules directly. These providers process data under their own privacy and security terms. Other content-source links open the named third-party website only when selected.

Retention and deletion

Local data remains until you delete books or personal data in Settings, clear the app’s storage, or uninstall the app. Cloud sync data and Amudi entitlement records remain while the account exists. In Settings → Account, “Delete account” first asks you to reauthenticate and then deletes the synchronized Firestore record, stored Amudi entitlement records, and Firebase Authentication account. Updated app versions then remove the permanent tester grant linked to your verified email. If that final request fails after the account is deleted, the device temporarily keeps a signed cleanup proof containing the former account ID and a hash of the email address, but not the email itself. It retries when Amudi next opens online; the proof expires after 24 hours. Contact support through the external deletion request if cleanup does not finish, particularly if you will not reopen the app. Local device data is separate and can be deleted with “Delete personal data.” You may also submit an external deletion request at Amudi’s account-deletion page. A tester email hash may remain in the original group eligibility list; contact support to remove it from that list. Amudi retains a minimal purchase-token hash and account-binding record to prevent a receipt from being claimed by multiple accounts, and a voided-receipt record for refunded or charged-back purchases. These are not public; contact support to request their deletion or review, including any applicable fraud-prevention retention requirement. Google may retain Play purchase records under Google’s own legal and accounting obligations. Verified requests are normally completed within 30 days unless limited retention is required for security, fraud prevention, or law; any such retained records are isolated and deleted when no longer required.

Your choices

You may read freely accessible material without a cloud account; paid purchases and permanent tester access require a verified Google-backed account. In versions with the automatic clock check, you may decline using the suggested time or zone, but that choice does not prevent the initial Time.Now request. You may decline notifications, export your local reading data, delete local personal data, sign out, or permanently delete the account. You may also request deletion of selected synchronized reading data while keeping the account through the data-only request path. Device and platform settings can also revoke notification permissions.

Security and children

Amudi uses HTTPS, Firebase security rules, Android Keystore protection for the library key, and authenticated package checks. No system can guarantee absolute security. Amudi is a general-audience study app and is not directed to children under 13; it does not knowingly seek children’s personal information. A parent or guardian may contact the address above about deletion.

Changes
